Systemic Circulation

What is Systemic Circulation?

Systemic circulation is the part of the cardiovascular system that carries oxygenated blood from the heart to the body and returns deoxygenated blood back to the heart.

Detailed Explanation

  1. Oxygenated Blood Leaves the Heart:

    • Blood returns to the left atrium of the heart via the pulmonary veins.
    • The left atrium pushes blood through the mitral valve into the left ventricle.

  2. Blood is Pumped to the Body:

    • Left ventricle pumps blood through the aortic valve into the aorta.
    • Aorta branches into arteries to supply the whole body.

  3. Oxygen is Delivered to the Tissues:

    • Arteries branch into arterioles and capillaries for oxygen exchange.
    • Capillaries exchange oxygen and nutrients with cells.

  4. Deoxygenated Blood Returns:

    • Venules collect blood and form veins.
    • Veins return blood to the right atrium via superior and inferior vena cava.

  5. The Cycle Continues:

    • Blood flows to the right ventricle and continues to pulmonary circulation.

Key Points of Systemic Circulation

Aorta:

The main artery that carries oxygen-rich blood from the left ventricle to the body.

Arteries and Capillaries:

Distribute oxygen and nutrients to the tissues.

Veins:

Return deoxygenated blood to the heart via the superior and inferior vena cava.
