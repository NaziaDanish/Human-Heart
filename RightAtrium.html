Right Atrium

The right atrium is one of the four chambers of the human heart, playing a crucial role in the circulation of deoxygenated blood. Located in the upper-right section of the heart, it receives oxygen-poor blood from the body through two major veins: the superior vena cava and the inferior vena cava...

Here’s a breakdown of how the right atrium works:

Working of the Right Atrium:

  • Receiving Deoxygenated Blood:
    • Superior Vena Cava: Brings blood from the upper body.
    • Inferior Vena Cava: Brings blood from the lower body.
    • Coronary Sinus: Drains blood from heart muscles.
  • Holding Blood: The atrium stretches to hold the incoming blood.
  • Atrial Contraction: Contracts and sends blood to the right ventricle.
  • Blood Flow Regulation: Tricuspid valve prevents backflow.

Summary of Blood Flow through the Right Atrium:

  • Step 1: Blood returns from the body to the right atrium.
  • Step 2: Atrium fills with deoxygenated blood.
  • Step 3: Atrium contracts and pushes blood to right ventricle.
  • Step 4: Blood goes to lungs for oxygenation.
